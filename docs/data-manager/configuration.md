# Configuration reference

The settings files are in `mapdata\config`, written in YAML. Each section below is one file or folder, with each setting and what it does. The guide, guide.md in this folder, says how the commands use them.

## paths.yaml

The local paths file. It is not in git, because it holds a folder on your own computer (AD-18). Copy `paths.example.yaml` to make it.

* data_folder: the data folder. mapdata reads source files there and writes only in its `tobymap` folder. The `--data` option and the MAPDATA_ROOT environment variable override it.

## sources.yaml

Every source a pack can be built from (DMT-6, DAT-9, DAT-11). Under `sources:`, each source has a key, such as land-polygons, which packs and commands use, and these settings:

* name: the source as the pack's manifest names it. Needed.
* licence: its licence, such as ODbL 1.0. Needed: a source without one stops every build.
* attribution: the credit its licence asks for, written into every pack that uses it. Needed.
* about: a sentence on what it is.
* url: where `mapdata fetch` downloads it from.
* file: where the download is kept, inside the downloads folder.
* find: for a source kept by hand, such as the planet file, where to look in the data folder, as patterns where `*` stands for any text. The newest match is used, by the date in its name, or failing that its file date.
* repo: a file or folder in the repository, such as the test samples.
* folder: a folder inside the downloads folder that a stage fills itself as it runs, such as wikidata, where the wikidata stage keeps the facts it fetches (P3-T2a). It need not exist before the build. Only its name is in the fingerprints, so filling it does not make the stage run again; what the stage reads from it is named by the stage's own settings. `mapdata fetch` lists it as fetched by the build itself.
* date: the data's own date, as year-month-day, when the file's date is not it. Repository files need one, as checking out the repository changes their file dates.
* refresh_days: how many days old the source can be before fetch says a newer copy is due.

Each source also needs either url and file, url and folder, find, or repo. Every source must also be in docs/design/data sources.md, with a licence compatible with sharing packs under the ODbL.

## layers

One file per group of layers, such as `layers\base.yaml`. Under `layers:`, each layer has:

* id: the layer's identifier, which areas name.
* name: the layer's name, as the application shows it.
* rank: orders what is at a point. Higher ranks come first, then smaller areas (LOC-2), so a county is said before its country. Continents are 1, countries and oceans 2, mountain ranges and physical regions 3, regions and seas 4, islands 5, counties 6, lakes 7, and rivers, peaks and other places 8.
* kind: land or water. The water stage takes the land out of water layers. Lakes and rivers are land in this sense: they lie on the land, and nothing is taken out of them.
* made_by: the stage that makes the layer, when it is not the grid squares and join, such as continents, osm-layer, osm-rivers or physical (P3-T1e). Such a layer is left out of the fingerprints of the stages that read the planet file's countries, build the grid squares and join them, so adding it, or changing it, does not run those stages again: about 3 hours for the world. A layer of areas the grid squares make must not have it. (none)

These say how the application shows and sounds the layer (SCP-7). They are written into the pack, and the user can change some of them in the layers list. Each may be left out, and then the default in brackets is used. Changing them makes the build write the pack again, but the stages before the writing are not run again, because they do not use them.

* group: the group the layer is listed under in the layers list, such as Boundaries or Water. (None: the list puts it under "Other layers".)
* geometry: area, line or point. (area)
* switched_on: true or false, whether the layer is on until the user changes it. The word "on" is not used, because YAML reads it as true. (true)
* min_zoom_m and max_zoom_m: the zoom range, as zoom distances in metres. The layer is visible from the closer zoom, min_zoom_m, to the farther one, max_zoom_m (NAV-13). (0, and no limit)
* min_size: for an area layer, how wide an area must be, as a share of the zoom distance, to be visible (DP80). (0.02, which is 2%)
* crossing_earcon: the sound played when a line of the layer is crossed, by its event name in mapcore/describe/earcons.yaml, such as line_crossing. (none)
* crossing_earcons: a sound of its own for some types of line in the layer, as each line type with its sound's event name, such as {equator: equator_crossed, prime-meridian: meridian_crossed}. A line of a type not named here plays crossing_earcon. If the user switches the layer's crossing sound off, every line of it is silent. The special-lines layer uses this, so the meridians, the equator, the tropics and the polar circles sound different from the date line. (none)
* sound: the sound of the layer's features in "hear its direction" and look around, by event name. (none)
* fill: for an area layer, how the map paints it: land, in the land colours; water, in the ocean's colour, under the land; or none, not painted at all, for a layer whose areas lie over the others, such as the continents and mountain ranges (P3-T1e). The areas of a layer with fill none are simplified one at a time at each drawing level, as they may overlap. Changing it rewrites only the pack. (land, but water for the oceans)
* line_style: for a line layer, plain or special. A special layer's lines are drawn thicker and in the theme's colour for special lines, on top of the plain lines. The special-lines layer uses this, so the equator, the prime meridian, the tropics, the polar circles and the date line stand out. Changing it rewrites only the pack, in the stage "writing the pack". (plain)
* shown_name: the name the application shows and says for the layer, when it is not its name. The name is part of every stage's fingerprint, so changing it builds every layer again from the planet file; changing shown_name rewrites only the pack. The Regions layer is shown as "Regions and constituent countries" this way (P3-T17). (the name)

The layer file `samples.yaml` holds one layer for the test pack only: railway stations (a point). The test pack's sample river and lake are in the base layers' rivers and lakes (P3-T1g, P3-T1h). The samples stage keeps the lines and points of a line or point layer as they are, and checks that a feature's shape fits its layer.

A setting the build does not know, a geometry other than the three, a closer zoom farther than the farther one, or a sound that is not one of the application's stops the build with the reason.

### The zoom ranges chosen (P2-T4b)

They start from the Shortbread schema (data sources, version 0.7), which says the zoom at which each kind of boundary first appears on a web map. A web map zoom is turned into a view radius: at web zoom 7 a 1,024 pixel wide map is about 1,250 km across, so about 600 km from the centre to the edge. TobyMap's zooms are a list, so the range ends on the nearest zoom in the list.

* countries: no limit. Shortbread draws country boundaries from the whole world down. They are the top of the hierarchy and are heard at every zoom.
* oceans: no limit, for the same reason.
* seas: no limit (P3-T1b). Shortbread names seas and bays as labels on its water from web zoom 0, and the largest, such as the Mediterranean Sea and the Bay of Biscay, are as wide as countries. The smallest are a few kilometres across; the size rule (min_size) leaves out what is too small to see at a zoom, as for every area layer.
* regions: visible to a zoom of 1,000 km (max_zoom_m 1000000). Shortbread draws boundaries at administrative level 4 from web zoom 7, a view radius of about 600 km. Farther out, the regions of large countries are too many to be useful in a list.
* counties: visible to a zoom of 100 km (max_zoom_m 100000). Shortbread draws level 6 boundaries from web zoom 10, a view radius of about 80 km.
* islands: visible to a zoom of 1,000 km (max_zoom_m 1000000), the same as regions (P3-T1d). An island sits between a region and a county in the hierarchy, so Tenerife is inside the Canary Islands region. Shortbread draws island names as labels from web zoom 7, a view radius of about 600 km.
* continents: no limit (P3-T1e). They are the top of the hierarchy on land, and are heard at every zoom, as the countries are. They are not painted on the map: the countries are.
* mountain ranges and physical regions: no limit (P3-T1f). Shortbread has neither. The largest, such as the Alps or the Sahara, are as wide as countries; the size rule leaves out what is too small to see at a zoom.
* peaks: visible to a zoom of 50 km (max_zoom_m 50000). Shortbread labels peaks from web zoom 11 or 12, a view radius of about 40 km. The trial has 73,821 peaks of 1,000 metres or more; at 100 km over the Alps, they took 61 ms of a map's 157 ms, and thousands would be listed.
* lakes: no limit (P3-T1h). Shortbread draws water areas from web zoom 0; the size rule leaves out lakes too small to see, so at a continent's zoom only lakes such as Lake Geneva are listed.
* rivers: visible to a zoom of 250 km (max_zoom_m 250000) (P3-T1g). Shortbread draws rivers from web zoom 9, a view radius of about 150 km; TobyMap keeps only rivers of 50 km or more, so the range ends on the next zoom farther out. At 1,000 km over the Alps, the rivers were 54,000 of a map's 69,000 points, and 66 ms of its 114 ms.
* graticule and special lines: no limit. The graticule thins itself by its interval setting (DAT-13), and the special lines are few. They are off at first.
* No layer has a minimum zoom yet: the closest zoom in the list is 100 m, and every layer is useful there.

Where TobyMap differs from Shortbread: it keeps countries at every zoom, because someone listening wants to hear the country however far out they are. Shortbread has no sounds, so there is no other difference yet. Changing a range makes the build write the pack again, which takes about 26 minutes for the world pack.

## packs

One file per pack, such as `packs\test.yaml`. The file name is the pack's name.

* name: the pack's name, the same as the file name.
* title: one sentence on what the pack is.
* version: the pack's version, such as 0.1.0.
* app_versions: the application versions it works with: from, and optionally before.
* coverage: what it covers: kind, world or region.
* layer_groups: the layer files it uses, by name.
* space_needed_mb: about how much disk space a build needs. The build stops before starting if there is less.
* stages: the stages, in order. Each starts with `stage:` and its kind, followed by its own settings.

### The stages

* samples: reads small GeoJSON files, for the test pack. Its setting samples lists them, least detailed first, each with source, the source's key, and file, for a file inside a source that is a folder. Each feature in a sample has id, name, layer and type, and may have local_name, parent, sovereign and replaces. A sample may have a box, a list of rectangles as west, south, east, north; inside it, the sample wins over the samples before it. tests/samples/README.md says more.
* water: takes the land out of every water layer. Its setting land_layer names the layer that is the land, countries if not given.
* osm-areas: reads the countries and their divisions from the planet file, in a short-lived process of its own. Settings: source (planet), levels (the division levels to read, such as 3, 4 and 5), and box (west, south, east, north), for a trial on one part of the world only. The world build has no box. With counties: true, it also reads the areas for the Counties layer (P3-T1c): for each country, the levels in the counties record, or 6, 7 and 8 where the record has none. With islands: true, it also reads every named island and islet (place=island or place=islet) from the planet: as areas where mapped as an area, and as a point where mapped only as a point (P3-T1d). This makes the planet file be read again, about 16 minutes for the world. The planet file is still read once. Each county area is set aside on disk, in a file for its country; then, a country at a time, in the worker processes, an area held twice is kept once (DP104), and an area at a later level with less than 1 km2 outside the levels before it is left out, as it can fill no gap. So the whole world's level 7 and 8 areas are never in memory at once. The setting counties_record names another counties record, as a path from the repository's top folder; the tests use it. Correcting a country's levels in the record makes the next build read the planet file again; a new date alone does not.
* squares: builds the world one grid square at a time, from the land polygons, the areas just read and the VLIZ oceans, and keeps each finished grid square, so a stopped build carries on from there. Settings: sources (land-polygons and vliz-oceans), square_degrees (1 by default), tolerance_m (the base detail in metres; the PO chose 100 on 3 November, DP112), box, and condominiums (land two countries hold together, each with id, name and the two countries), counties: true for a Counties layer by the rule of DP101 (P3-T1a), and seas: the source of the named seas, such as naturalearth-marine-areas, for the Seas, bays and straits layer (P3-T1b, DP168). With seas, all water is in the smallest named sea that covers it, as well as its ocean; Natural Earth's own oceans and unnamed areas name no sea, and its reefs are left out. Natural Earth's coasts are rough, so water between a sea's edge and the real coast takes the sea beside it, the one it shares the longest edge with. With counties, all land is in its country's smallest level 6 area; where level 6 leaves a gap, the level 7 area, then the level 8 area; and where none covers it, the region itself, as "region used as county". A country's levels come from the counties record, mapdata/config/county_levels.yaml, when it has them. The osm-areas stage before must have counties: true as well. A county is never the region itself: where a country's counties are level 5 and a region comes from level 5, the next level is used there. The join stage gives a region used as county over less than 1 km2 in all, a sliver or an islet, to the county beside it or nearest.
* join: joins the grid squares into whole areas, gives land in no country to its neighbour or names it "Unclaimed", gives water in no ocean to the ocean beside it, works out the colour numbers, and checks that every quarter of a degree is in a country or a sea (LOC-6). Settings: sliver_width_m and sliver_km2 (what counts as a sliver), unclaimed (known unclaimed land, each with name, point and note) and named_waters (water with no ocean beside it, such as the Caspian Sea, each with id, name and point). With counties, it lists in the log and in the quality report each country's county levels, the land each holds, every gap filled by a later level, and every region used as county; then it writes the counties record for each country the build read whole, marked "worked out by the build of" the pack and the date. A country cut by the reading box is not written. A corrected entry is never overwritten, and an entry that says the same as what was found keeps its date.
* continents (P3-T1e, DAT-12, DP174): adds the seven continents, built from the Countries layer, in the layer continents. Its settings are in mapdata/config/continents.yaml, not in the pack's file, and they are part of its fingerprint. There, each continent has one or more areas, as lists of points (longitude, latitude), taken in order: a place in two belongs to the first, and the continent with rest: true has every place that no area holds, so together they cover the world once. A list of points may name a line given under lines, such as ural-river, to put its points in that place. A country goes whole to the continent whose area holds most of its land; a group of its parts more than distant_km (300) from the rest, such as the Canary Islands, goes to the continent whose area holds most of that group (DP88). A country named under split, by its Wikidata link, is cut along the edges of the areas instead: Russia, Kazakhstan, Turkey, Egypt, Georgia, Azerbaijan and Indonesia. A part cut off smaller than split_min_km2 (100) stays with the rest. The log lists every country with land in more than one continent, with how much is in each. For the whole world, it takes about 12 minutes.
* osm-layer (P3-T1h): reads named areas of at least a size from the planet file into a layer of their own, in a short-lived process, as osm-areas does. Settings: source (planet), layer (such as lakes), queries (each with query, in GeoDesk's query language, and type, the type its features get), min_km2 (smaller areas are left out), tolerance_m (how far a simplified outline may move), box, and check, a HydroLAKES source key, with check_km2 and check_within_km. With check, each HydroLAKES lake of check_km2 or more is looked for: a lake of the layer within check_within_km of its point, or one with its name, ignoring accents, within 30 km, as HydroLAKES puts the point where a lake flows out, sometimes kilometres down its river. The log lists those not found. The lakes are read with natural=water and water=lake, reservoir, lagoon or pond, or no water tag, and landuse=reservoir.
* osm-rivers (P3-T1g): joins the planet file's river ways into whole rivers, in a short-lived process. Each waterway relation of type river with a Wikidata link is a river, with all its ways; a relation with no link of its own takes the link most of its ways carry. Each river way with a Wikidata link in no such relation joins the river with that link. A relation with no link at all is a river too, if it is at least min_km_unlinked long (200 km), such as Brazil's Rio Verde. A relation whose ways are nine tenths in a larger river's, such as the Upper Rhine in the Rhine, is a section of it and is left out, and the log says so. A river's ways are joined end to end where they meet, keeping the direction each is drawn in, which in OpenStreetMap is the way the water flows; a river with branches, such as a delta, is one feature with several lines. Settings: source (planet), layer (rivers), min_km (shorter rivers, measured along all their lines, are left out: 50 by DP169), tolerance_m, box (a trial keeps only the lines inside it), and check, the key of Natural Earth's rivers, with check_km and check_within_km: each Natural Earth river of check_km or more inside the box is looked for, by its Wikidata link, by its name in a river within 50 km, ignoring accents and words such as River and Rio, or by a river within check_within_km of the middle of its longest line, and the log lists those not found. A river OpenStreetMap has only as ways with no Wikidata link, in no relation, is not read: joining every named river way in the world by name would need about ten times the memory. Such rivers are the ones the check lists, and the place to put them right is OpenStreetMap.
* physical (P3-T1f, DAT-5): adds mountain ranges and physical regions, cut to the land with the land polygons, so at sea you are never in a mountain range. Settings: sources (gmba-mountains, naturalearth-physical-regions and land-polygons, for the fingerprint), box, and two parts, each with source, layer and tolerance_m. ranges: GMBA's ranges from min_level to max_level of its hierarchy (3 to 7), leaving out its support polygons, islands, archipelagos and continents, and its grouped sub-ranges, such as "Southeast Europe", above sub_range_min_level (4). Each range names the nearest range above it that is kept as its parent, so Within can list the Bernese Alps, the Western Alps and the Alps. regions: Natural Earth's physical regions of the classes given under classes, each with the type it is called, such as Desert: desert; the others are left out.
* graticule: adds the lines of latitude and longitude at every whole degree (layer graticule, each marked with the intervals it belongs to: 1, 5, 10, 15 or 30 degrees) and the special lines (layer special-lines): the equator, the prime meridian, the 180 degree meridian, the tropics, the polar circles and the international date line. The 0 degree and 180 degree lines are special lines only. Every line has its points at most 1 degree apart, and keeps all of them in the drawing levels. Settings: year (needed: the tropics and polar circles are worked out for it), source (the key of the Natural Earth geographic lines, for the date line; without it the date line is left out), and layer and special_layer (the layers' ids, graticule and special-lines). Put it before write.
* write: writes the pack, with its manifest, attribution, licence and checksum. Its setting drawing_levels is a list of levels, each with tolerance_m (how far the outline may move), min_km2 (parts smaller than this are left out, but each area keeps its largest part), tile_level (the tile level of its drawing tiles) and view_km (it serves views at least this wide). Each layer is simplified as one coverage at each level, so neighbours meet at every level; a layer with fill none is simplified one area at a time. Without drawing_levels the pack has none. Every id in a pack must be different: an id already used by a feature of another layer, such as an island that is also a region in OpenStreetMap, is written with the ending "#as-" and its layer's id, and one used twice in a layer with "#2"; the log lists each.
* osm-places, for peaks (P3-T1f): a query with elevation: true keeps each place's height in metres from OSM's ele tag, as elevation_m. A height that is not a plain number, such as "about 4000" or "4,808", is left out.
* osm-places, for towns and cities (P3-T1j): a query with population: true keeps each place's population from OSM's population tag, as population, with population_source OpenStreetMap, until Wikidata's replaces it (P3-T2). A population with spaces, commas or dots between the thousands, such as "12 345" or "1.234.567", is read; one that is not a whole number, such as "about 5000", is left out. The setting within lists area layers, such as continents, countries, regions, islands and counties: each place keeps the ids of the areas of those layers it is in, in that order, as within, for search's descriptions. A place in none of them, such as a harbour town whose point is in the harbour, has the nearest area of each layer within 1 km. The writing stage gives each the id the area is written with, if an id had to be changed to keep every id different.
* osm-lines (P3-T1i): reads roads and railways from the planet file's ways, in a short-lived process, and joins them into whole lines, so a road is one feature, not thousands of short lines. Settings: source (planet), tolerance_m, box, and layers, a list, each with layer (such as roads), queries (each with query and type, such as motorway; a way found by an earlier query keeps that query's type), key (ref: joined by number, such as M3, then by name for ways with no number; name: joined by name, then by number), unnamed (the name of lines with neither) and number_name (for key name, the words before a number alone, such as "Railway line 5919"). Only the first of several numbers, such as "A 3;IP 1", is used. The ways of one key are joined per country, the one the middle of each way is in, or the nearest, from the Countries layer of the stage before; so the A 7 in Germany and the A 7 in Austria are two roads. A way with neither number nor name takes the key of the ways it meets end to end, again and again, so a short link joins its road; the rest are one line per country, called by unnamed. A road with a number is named by its number, with the name most of its ways carry added when at least half carry it: "A-8, Autovia del Cantabrico". A line's type and Wikidata link are those most of its ways have. A closed line, such as a roundabout, keeps its ring when simplified. Motorways are mapped as two carriageways, and many railways as one way per track, so each line can have two or more lines side by side; its length in the log counts them all.
* disputes (P3-T2c, ANN-6, DAT-8, DP119): makes the disputed areas. First, land two countries both hold becomes a disputed area of its own in the Countries layer, type "disputed land", with both countries as claimants, in the order of their names, and each country keeps the rest of its land, so every point is still in one country (LOC-6). The pairs come from the join's list of land held twice, and the land from the outlines as read by osm-areas: the land in both outlines that a country of the pack holds. Without those stages, as in the test pack, it is the land where two countries of the pack overlap. A place in that land, such as a town in Crimea, has the disputed area in place of its country among the areas it is in. Second, Natural Earth's breakaway and disputed areas mark the areas of the pack inside them: an area of the layers in mark with at least share of its land inside one is marked disputed, with Natural Earth's name for it and its note, written in full words, as the source's view ("Administered by Russia. Claimed by Ukraine."). Only Natural Earth's areas whose note names a claim are used, and those named in include. Last, an area OpenStreetMap holds twice in one layer (DP104) stays disputed only if it is in disputed land; the others, such as a town in Alberta mapped twice, keep the other copy's name but are not said as disputed. Settings: source (naturalearth-disputed), held_twice (names for the land held twice, each with countries, the two ids, id, name and wikidata; land with no name here is called "Land claimed by" the two countries), min_km2 (land held twice that is smaller is left as it is, 0.01), mark (countries, regions, islands and counties), share (0.9), include (such as Kosovo, which Natural Earth gives no note), and outlines (the stage whose outlines are used, osm-areas). Put it after the stages that make features, and before wikidata.
* wikidata (P3-T2a, DAT-7): gives each feature with a Wikidata link its facts from Wikidata: population with its date, area, capital, elevation, length, official name, and the English Wikipedia article's title, each with its source, the date fetched, and the fact's own date where Wikidata gives one, cut to its precision, such as 2020 for a population of that year. Wikidata's population replaces OpenStreetMap's. The facts are fetched from Wikidata's query service, 200 items to a query with a second between queries, and kept in the source's folder as facts-<refresh>.jsonl, a line for each item with every statement Wikidata gave, so a rebuild fetches nothing again and gives the same pack (DMT-1). Which statement is used is chosen when the stage runs: the preferred ones if any, then the latest; so changing that rule needs no fetching. A stopped fetch carries on where it stopped. Settings: source (wikidata, or sample-wikidata for the test pack), refresh (the label of the facts file, such as 2026-11: a new label fetches everything afresh into a new file), links (a feature's link given by hand, by its id, which wins over its own and over links.yaml in the source's folder), match (layers whose features with no link are matched by name and place: an item with the same English name, with coordinates within match_km, 50 by default, of the feature's box; only where exactly one is found, and the log lists each), offline (true to fetch nothing, as for the test pack) and batch. The build's lines give, for each layer, how many features have a link, facts and a Wikipedia article, and DAT-7's two measures: countries, and cities over 100,000 people, with a population and a Wikipedia article. Put it after the stages that make features and before write.
* relationships (P3-T3, AD-8): works out each feature's relationships, which the writing stage puts in the pack: the areas that hold it fully, from every layer, so the parents of parents too ("in"); two areas that are the same land, each inside the other to within same_share ("same", DP71); areas of any layers that overlap, neither holding the other, with the share of each ("overlaps", INF-5); for a line or a place, the areas it is partly inside, with the share ("partly"); areas of one layer that share a border, with its length in km, longest first ("next", INF-2), and those that nearly touch, within near_km, with the distance ("near"); for a line, the areas it passes through in order from its start, which for a river is its source ("crosses", INF-6); for an area, the lines crossing it ("crossed_by") and those along its border, at least a kilometre within border_m of it ("along"); for a water area, the water area of a lower rank that holds most of it, at least water_share ("water_in", DAT-4); and for an area, how many features of each layer are inside it, fully or partly ("counts"). It never works on a whole layer at once: the world is cut into boxes of top_deg degrees, each split in four while its parts hold more than leaf_points points, down to min_leaf_deg, and only the parts in each box are compared; an area covering a box whole is noted, not cut. A stretch of an area's own outline lying on a cutting line, such as the border of Montana and Wyoming along 45 degrees north, is recorded with the side it is on and matched across the line. A river in several parts is measured along its longest part. The top boxes are jobs for the workers. Settings: same_share (0.99), full_share (0.995: an area's border is simplified, so a little of a county may fall outside its country), partly_share (0.005: less is taken for a coast or border drawn slightly differently in two sources), cross_km (0.05: a line crosses an area when at least this much of it is inside), near_km (5), near_share (0.25) and near_most (10) (two areas nearly touch when no further apart than near_share of the smaller one's width, the square root of its area, and never more than near_km; each keeps at most the near_most nearest: a flat 5 km gave 1.25 million pairs on the Western Europe trial, nearly all islets of archipelagos), border_m (100), water_share (0.5), leaf_points (20000), min_leaf_deg (0.25), top_deg (45), along_lines and along_areas (the layers looked at for a line along a border, as buffering every outline is slow: rivers, against countries, regions and counties), and leave_out (layers with no relationships: graticule and special-lines). Put it just before write.
* checks: looks up the check places in the pack just written. It must come straight after write. Settings: places (the files in tests/acceptance), box, line_180 (true to check both sides of the 180 degree line, and that no edge jumps across the globe), and area_checks (countries compared with Wikidata's areas, each with name, wikidata_km2, tolerance_pct and note).

The write stage must come last, or be followed only by checks.

## Other files

These are used by spike S2's commands, which stay until task P1-T16 is finished:

* s2_stage1.yaml to s2_stage4.yaml: the spike S2 stages.
* country_units.yaml: the areas that replace a level 2 country in the Countries layer, such as French Guiana.
* county_levels.yaml: the counties record, which levels make each country's Counties layer, in order, and which regions are used as their own county. The build writes it; to correct a country, change its levels and start its "how" with "corrected: " and the reason. Argentina, Tunisia and South Sudan are corrected to level 5 first (P3-T1c).
* tiers.yaml, names (DP181): what each country calls its tiers, such as state for the United States' regions and province for Canada's, with any area named on its own, such as Yukon, a territory. `mapdata tier-names <pack>` writes it from the kinds the pack's wikidata stage kept: each area's kinds from Wikidata cut to the words before " of " or " in ", so "state of the United States" and "U.S. state" are both "state"; kinds that say nothing about the tier, such as "administrative territorial entity", left out; the word most of a country's areas in a layer share, if at least half do, names the layer, and an area without it keeps its own. Countries already in the section, and those with their own tiers in the record, such as the United Kingdom, are kept as they are, so correct a name there by hand. The writing stage gives each area its name, and Details says it in place of the tier's word.
* tiers.yaml: the tiers record, beside the counties record (P3-T17, DP165). The writing stage reads it to give each area of the countries, regions and counties layers its tier, such as constituent country for England. It lists the tiers highest first; each layer's tier; a country's own tier for a layer, such as the United Kingdom's regions as constituent countries; and an area's own tier, under "areas". A region used as its own county takes its region's tier. Every tier given must be in the list, and needs its words in mapcore/describe/templates.yaml, under details, tiers. Changing the record writes the pack again, but builds nothing else again.

## The run switch

continue.txt, in the repository's top folder, is not a settings file, but builds read it: a first line of yes lets a build carry on, and anything else, such as no, pauses it (the PO, 10 November 2026). A missing file means carry on. The MAPDATA_SWITCH environment variable can name another file. The guide says more.
