# Phase 1 task list

Phase 1 is the walking skeleton. The tasks are described in "implementation plan.md", section 4, version 0.4, and in detail in phase-1-plan.md, which also raises DP110 to DP114. This list shows what is done.

Started on 3 November 2026, after the PO accepted Phase 0.

## Tasks

* [x] P1-T1 Core geodesy: distance, bearing, stepping, the poles, the 180 degree line, and formatting. Done 3 November on branch p1-t1-geodesy; macOS is confirmed by continuous integration when it is pushed.
* [x] P1-T2 Pack format version 1: the writer and reader, the byte source, the manifest, checksums and version checks. Done 3 November on branch p1-t2-pack-format. Its format number is 2. The spike format, 1, was dropped at your request: the spike S2 stages, their comparison program and the spike S4 timings now use format 2, and mapdata inspect reads only format 2. The specification is docs/developer/pack-format.md. The browser tests read a pack in Pyodide in Chromium and Firefox; macOS is confirmed by continuous integration when it is pushed.
* [x] P1-T3 Pipeline skeleton: check, fetch and build commands, configuration, stage caching, progress, summary and log. Done 3 November on branch p1-t3-pipeline. The test pack is built from about 4 MB of samples in tests/samples, in 4 seconds, and continuous integration builds it on every push. Also added: the run switch, so every build reads continue.txt before each stage, stops cleanly on "no", and with --wait-for-yes carries on by itself on "yes". Of spike S2's code, only the pack writer is a pipeline stage so far; reading the planet file, cutting to the land and the checks become stages in P1-T16 and P1-T4, as they are rebuilt for the world. Stage results are kept as plain JSON and WKB files rather than GeoParquet, which would need a large new library. VLIZ's oceans hold only 10 ocean regions, not the named seas, so DP115 asks where those should come from. The guide is docs/data-manager/guide.md, with docs/data-manager/configuration.md.
* [x] P1-T4 Base pack "lite": countries, first-level divisions, oceans and seas, and land, with no gaps (LOC-6). Also, from P1-T16: Antarctica's area against Wikidata, and the globe check near the 180 degree line only. In progress on branch p1-t4-base-lite. The world build ran on 4 November 2026 with 8 workers: the planet file in 4 minutes, the 28,142 grid squares that are not open sea in 46 minutes, the join in 3 minutes, and the write and checks in 25 minutes. The pack is world.pack in tobymap\output\world, 125.9 MB at 100 metres (DP112), with 246 countries, 3,207 first-level divisions and 11 oceans. All 1,036,800 points, every quarter of a degree, are in a country or a sea. All 89 check places pass, and all 7 other checks: Antarctica's area is 13,951,007 km2 against Wikidata's 13,720,000, 14,000,000 and 14,200,000; both sides of the 180 degree line are named and agree at all 720 latitudes; no edge jumps across the globe; Fiji, Russia and the United States are within 3.4% of Wikidata and Kiribati is listed as explained (DP118). Three drawing levels (4,000, 1,000 and 150 kilometres across) are written. Faults found and fixed on the way: grid squares whose shared edges were noded differently (the join stopped), water in no ocean (hours, now seconds), the no-gaps check (hours, now 40 seconds), Antarctica (no country in OSM, so 7,600 false "Unclaimed" islands, now one territory), and the ellipsoid area of oceans (hours, now seconds). New discussion points: DP119 (land in two countries, 15 places) and DP120 (Antarctica and unclaimed land). The write stage built a second time from the kept results (tools/build_twice.py) gives a pack with the same checksum, so DMT-1 holds for the world pack. Done 4 November. You accepted the pack's size, 125.9 MB, and answered DP119 and DP120. DP119: the rule for land in two countries stays as it is until Phase 3 can mark an area as disputed; a way to list or search disputed areas is to be added at a later phase. DP120: Antarctica stays as built; all other land no country holds stays "Unclaimed" in Phase 1, with a way to list or search unclaimed areas later; the West Bank and the Ilemi Triangle are marked disputed land rather than unclaimed in the join stage and the settings (4 November), but the world pack has not been rebuilt yet, so it still says Unclaimed until the next world build, and a way to list or search all disputed and all unclaimed areas is task P3-T16. DP112: your question about very short ways is answered; the size report is docs/plan/p1-t4-size-report.md.
* [x] P1-T5 Store and "where am I": point queries, the pack tile cache, and the nearest point of an area for Nearby (INF-3, INF-7). Done 4 November on branch p1-t5-store (not yet committed). The code is mapcore/store. "What is at this point" reads the pack tile at each tile level and lists every area, a county before a region before a country, then smallest first (LOC-2). Nearby gives each area its nearest point, with the distance and bearing measured on the ellipsoid; at the Canadian side of Niagara Falls the USA is 0.26 miles away, to the east. Lookups work on both sides of the 180 degree line and at the South Pole. The pack tile cache is limited to 96 MB and can be filled round a position in advance. A damaged record is noted and the store carries on (NFR-4). Timings: on the test pack "where am I" took 2.4 ms with nothing cached and 0.04 ms cached; on the world pack, 75 ms for the first lookup at Niagara Falls, 5 to 7 ms elsewhere with nothing cached, and under 1 ms cached; the 20 nearest areas took 15 to 42 ms. The same checks run in Pyodide in Chromium and Firefox (mapcore/store/selfcheck.py); the web worker now also loads Shapely. FileSource now reads under a lock, so a background thread and the main thread can share a pack. Explained in explanation.md, "How where am I and Nearby find places in a pack". Still to do in later tasks: Nearby's wording (P1-T7); the web version's own pre-reading between steps (P1-T11).
* [x] P1-T6 World model and stepping, north-fixed. Done 4 November. The code is mapcore/world: location, heading (north), zoom and step lists from defaults.yaml, the link both ways, north-fixed stepping across the 180 degree line and blocked at the poles, joined moves, and save and restore. Tests: tests/mapcore/test_world_state.py. Not yet wired into the session; P1-T7 and P1-T8 do that.
* [x] P1-T7 Describe: announcements, coordinates, heading, repeat and history. Nearby measured to each area's nearest point, with the Niagara Falls test.
* [x] P1-T8 Command registry and session: F1 help, the key describer, remapping, clash tests, and F2, F3 and F4 to the regions (DP106).
* [x] P1-T9 Map scene: the projection, the shape list, the centre cross, the heading arrow and the current outline.
* [x] P1-T10 Desktop shell: the four regions, settings, and the stand-in audio. No backlog of speech after a held key (ANN-3), and the panel's levels read by NVDA (INF-10). On 5 November the PO heard the right levels from NVDA in a test window with our own tree (DP135, tools/spikes/tree_level/harness.py); the Information panel itself still uses Qt's tree, which NVDA always reads as level 1, until it is changed over.
* [x] P1-T11 Web shell: the same, in the browser, with the held key test.
* [x] P1-T12 Earcons.
* [x] P1-T13 Listening test tool.
* [ ] P1-T14 Packaging: the installer, the portable zip, the web version on GitHub Pages, and the first GitHub release when the phase is accepted. Most of it is built (installer without a pack, self-test option, continuous integration install steps, a Pages workflow that runs only by hand, release notes); left: the installer with the world pack, the continuous integration installer run, the Pages deployment, and the release at your acceptance (DP110).
* [x] P1-T15 Diagnostics: the test trace and "copy diagnostics".
* [x] P1-T16 Carried over from spike S2: build speed (DP107), areas held twice (DP104), inland water in the area check, and the wider overlap check. Done 3 November on branch p1-t16-build-speed. Of four methods for the counties step, gap-land is the fastest: mainland France's counties take 2.2 minutes, against more than 15 with spike S2's method, with the same counties. Argentina, which the method was not tuned on, took 2.3 to 2.5 minutes for 5.1 times France's land, with the same counties as nearby-gap, and showed the counties rule misses level 5 (DP116). No other step on Belgium was slow enough for its own trials. Each country is built and kept on its own, and each pair of neighbours' border is checked again only when one of them is rebuilt: a second Belgium run with nothing changed took 6 seconds instead of 48, and a change for Luxembourg rebuilt only Luxembourg. Areas held twice keep the country's own copy, marked disputed: Russia's Sevastopol, from stage 3's data. The overlap check also finds one area wholly inside another. The area check takes away inland water: Kiribati is 6.1% over Wikidata's figure without its water, against 35% with it. Two questions came out of it: the German and Luxembourg condominium on the Moselle, 7 km2 that OSM puts in both countries (DP117), and Kiribati's remaining difference (DP118). Mainland France's regression run is dropped at the PO's request, because France was used to develop the method. Stage 1 again and stage 4 (Argentina) are dropped at the PO's request (3 November): stage 1 again would repeat mainland France's regression test, already dropped, and Argentina's counties were already checked in the speed trials. Two smaller items move to P1-T4, as they are part of the world build: Antarctica's area against Wikidata, and the globe check looking only at edges near the 180 degree line. The counties rule using level 5 (DP116) waits for the first pack with a counties layer, as the base pack lite has none. Everything is written up in the S2 findings, "Phase 1: build speed trials".

## Suggested order

1. P1-T1 and P1-T2, the core's geodesy and pack format, which everything else uses.
2. P1-T3, the pipeline skeleton.
3. P1-T16's build speed work, on Belgium, with Argentina as the check on a country not used to tune it (DP107). Done.
4. P1-T4, the base pack, built with the faster methods.
5. P1-T5 to P1-T9, the core's store, stepping, wording, keys and map scene.
6. P1-T10 and P1-T11, the two shells, then P1-T12 and P1-T13, the sounds and the listening test.
7. P1-T15, diagnostics, then P1-T14, packaging, last.
