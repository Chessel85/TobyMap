When sourcing physical data for a map project, it helps to distinguish between data licensing (how geodata like shapefiles and rasters are licensed) and software licensing (like the GPL). Software licenses like GPL v2/v3 govern code rather than data.
Data is typically governed by open data licenses like Public Domain / CC0, CC-BY (Attribution), or ODbL (Open Database License, which OSM uses). Because you are building an accessible software application, all of these open data sources can be safely bundled with or ingested by a GPL-licensed codebase without violating copyright or software license terms.
Here are the best, fully open/permissively licensed physical geography data sources that fill in the gaps where OpenStreetMap falls short:
1. Natural Earth Data (Best for Macro Vector Features)
• 
License: Public Domain (No restrictions whatsoever—100% compatible with GPL software and any downstream data usage).
• 
Why it fits your project: It is specifically engineered by cartographers to provide clean, world-scale physical features that OSM often lacks in a unified format.
• 
Physical Layers Provided:
• 
Major mountain ranges, peaks, and physical region labels (e.g., "Himalayas", "Canadian Shield", "Amazon Basin").
• 
Rivers, lakes, playae, and coastal features categorized by scale rank.
• 
Land cover polygons, glaciers, and ocean bathymetry.
• 
Accessible Atlas Advantage: Because Natural Earth has structured scale ranks (1:10m, 1:50m, 1:110m), you can query simplified geometries and localized physical labels dynamically without overwhelming a screen reader or tactile/audio feedback engine with excessive vertex counts.
2. Copernicus DEM / GEBCO (Best for Elevation & Terrain)
If your accessible atlas needs to convey elevation changes, mountain passes, terrain contours, or bathymetry (ocean depth), open elevation datasets are essential:
• 
Copernicus DEM (GLO-30 / GLO-90):
• 
License: Free & Open Data Policy (Open for commercial and non-commercial use).
• 
Details: High-resolution global Digital Elevation Model derived from radar satellite data. GLO-90 provides a 90-meter resolution globally, perfect for processing regional terrain profiles.
• 
GEBCO (General Bathymetric Chart of the Oceans):
• 
License: Public Domain / Open Data Policy.
• 
Details: Provides global terrain models for both land and sea floor. If your map needs to represent underwater topography (trenches, continental shelves, ocean basins), GEBCO is the global standard.
3. USGS HydroSHEDS & HydroRIVERS (Best for Hydrological Networks)
• 
License: Free for redistribution and open use (Attribution requested).
• 
Why it fits your project: While OSM has good coverage of major rivers, its drainage network topology is often broken or fragmented across regional boundaries.
• 
Details: HydroSHEDS provides seamless vector line networks of stream and river topologies across the globe, ranked by flow volume and discharge. This makes it straightforward to extract only the major primary rivers globally for an accessible summary map.
4. Marine Regions (Best for Oceans, Seas & Bays)
• 
License: CC-BY 4.0 (Creative Commons Attribution).
• 
Why it fits your project: Standardizes names and bounding polygons for marine bodies of water.
• 
Details: Provides clean polygon vector boundaries for named seas, gulfs, bays, straits, and ocean basins (e.g., "Labrador Sea", "English Channel", "Bay of Bengal") which OSM often only represents as loose text nodes or vast, hard-to-query relations.
Integrating Open Data with your C++/Qt Application
Since your atlas uses C++ and Qt with spatial data libraries:
1. 
Licensing Isolation: Keeping data in a separate file store (e.g., a SpatiaLite or SQLite database containing Natural Earth/HydroSHEDS datasets) ensures clean legal separation. The ODbL (OSM) and Public Domain (Natural Earth) layers can coexist side-by-side as distinct tables.
2. 
Preprocessing Pipeline: You can use command-line open tools like GDAL/OGR (GPL/X11 licensed) to convert shapefiles or GeoTIFFs from these datasets into your spatial database format (SpatiaLite).
3. 
Hierarchy for Accessibility: Natural Earth's ne_10m_geographical_regions_polys and ne_10m_geographical_regions_points are particularly useful for tactile/audio labeling. They give precise centroids and bounding areas for macro physical zones (like the Altiplano or Gobi Desert) that OSM lacks unified tags for.

Yes, the data provided by Marine Regions is fully compliant and safe to use alongside a GPL-licensed application.
How the Licensing Works
1. 
Marine Regions Data License: Marine Regions distributes its vector products under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license (CC BY 4.0).   
View source details for citation from Marine Regions. Press Enter to open sources dialog.
2. 
Data vs. Code Legal Boundaries: Copyright law and open-source licensing treat data files (like SQLite/SpatiaLite database tables or Shapefiles) differently from executable application code. As long as your C++/Qt code reads the data externally from disk (or bundles it as standard database asset files) rather than hardcoding the raw spatial geometries directly into your C++ source header files, your GPL software license and the CC BY 4.0 data license do not conflict.
Requirements for Compliance
To remain 100% compliant with their CC BY 4.0 terms, you only need to meet two simple attribution conditions:
• 
Provide Attribution: Include an attribution statement in your application’s About, Credits, or documentation section.
• 
Example: "Marine boundary data provided by MarineRegions.org under CC BY 4.0 (Flanders Marine Institute)".
• 
Maintain License Notice: Retain a reference or link to the CC BY 4.0 license in your dataset metadata or app documentation.
You do not need to alter your C++/Qt codebase license, and you can freely package or distribute the .sqlite/.db files alongside your executable.
